# Agency website benchmark and search-intent research

Research date: October 12, 2026. This is a source-backed design and messaging study, not an audit of competitors' businesses. All links below were opened through the web research tool. The five requested URLs were attempted first. Competitors' awards, clients, certifications and results are self-published claims; their presence is a credibility-design observation, not independent verification or permission to reproduce them.

## Scope and evidence standard

The core benchmark contains 25 actual agencies: 10 established international comparators, 10 boutique/mid-market specialist comparators, and 5 smaller or independent design candidates. Groups describe comparative positioning, not verified employee counts, ownership or revenue. Large integrated agencies are included as performance/experience comparators and are not represented as affiliate-only specialists. Two additional requested references are documented separately.

**T** means actual website text/link extraction inspected. **V** means a rendered desktop homepage screenshot also inspected at approximately 1265 pixels wide. Text extraction establishes content structure, not typography, palette, visual quality, working interactions or actual rendering. Where these are not visible, they are explicitly **not assessed (NA)**. Mobile interaction, Lighthouse, Core Web Vitals, technical indexing, conversion rates and accessibility compliance were not measured for any competitor. A title/H1 or useful service page is only an observable SEO signal, not an SEO audit.

Scores are provisional **desktop hero design scores out of 10** (except the explicitly labeled RevWise section score), not business-quality ratings or complete-site scores: hierarchy/readability 3; specificity 2; restraint/brand coherence 3; visible CTA clarity 2. Numeric scores are assigned only to rendered references. NA is deliberately used rather than inventing a score. Five smaller/independent candidates were researched; the evidence does **not** justify calling every candidate an outstanding design. Growann, Folium and Hamster Garage are the strongest visually inspected smaller/independent references.

The ten requested criteria are numbered in each entry: **1** homepage/design, **2** branding/type, **3** palette, **4** navigation/architecture, **5** hero, **6** services, **7** trust, **8** case evidence, **9** conversion, **10** mobile/SEO/performance. The limitations above apply to every criterion 10.

## Five selected inspirations: what to adopt and avoid

These are a practical design shortlist, not an unsupported ranking of all 25 rendered sites. All five have rendered desktop evidence; AP was first selected for its information design, then visually checked. Silverbean remains a useful additional reference for buyer-stage navigation.

1. **[Growann](https://www.growann.com/) — editorial hierarchy (visual).** Adopt comfortable reading width, clear specialization, a useful explanation of the channel and visible next action. Avoid its exact split composition, font pairing, colors, illustration, client names and outcome data. Our identity should use original navy/off-white/blue tokens and original artwork.
2. **[Folium](https://www.foliumagency.com/) — typographic restraint (visual).** Adopt confidence through scale, spacing and few choices. Avoid neon-green identity, decorative particles, marketplace marks implying an affiliation, and developer/app navigation without an actual product.
3. **[iAffiliate Management](https://www.iaffiliatemanagement.com/) — specialist confidence (visual).** Adopt immediate audience qualification and a quiet header. Avoid long centered paragraphs, condensed all-caps everywhere and claims about senior staffing until verified.
4. **[Hamster Garage](https://www.hamstergarage.com/) — restrained specialist identity (visual).** Adopt generous negative space, a focused statement and a quiet original graphic. Avoid its exact near-black identity, vertical glow treatment and proprietary content; make navigation and the primary CTA larger than the small targets observed here.
5. **[Acceleration Partners](https://www.accelerationpartners.com/) — evidence architecture (text and visual).** Adopt challenge → work → measurement context for eventual case studies, plus services linked to relevant proof. Avoid copying metrics, client logos, proprietary technology language or enterprise navigation density. Adopt the clear primary/secondary CTA contrast, but avoid the observed wrapping navigation and outlined headline treatment.

Implementation consequence: a restrained editorial homepage, compact navigation, one primary conversation CTA, a secondary services CTA, four service families, a transparent operating process, and clearly labeled illustrative engagement briefs until approved case studies exist. The website must describe management of the client's program; publisher operations or paid acquisition should have separate scope and economics.

### Content priorities

1. **Before you launch an affiliate program: a commercial readiness checklist.** Informational → affiliate service. Cover margin, conversion readiness, returns, tracking, commission ceilings and team capacity. No promise that every brand should launch.
2. **Publisher recruitment is only the beginning: building a partner activation plan.** Informational/commercial → partnerships. Cover relevance, outreach, onboarding, usable assets, live placements and quality review.
3. **Affiliate-attributed revenue versus incremental growth.** Measurement intent → consultation. Explain distinction and experimental limits without pretending a dashboard establishes causation.
4. **What an affiliate program manager does each month.** Outsourcing intent → affiliate services. Give a practical operating calendar and ownership checklist.
5. **Paid media and affiliate marketing: setting different budgets and expectations.** Comparison intent → performance page. Separate click/media costs, publisher commission, network costs and agency fees.
6. **Expanding a partner program into another market.** Growth intent → scoped international support. Cover customer demand, localization, partner relevance, payment/currency and local professional review when needed.

Write the first two as original practical articles. Link every article to one relevant service and a genuinely useful next action. Use descriptive titles, short summaries, author/reviewer fields that remain unpublished until real people approve them, updated dates only when reviewed, and useful headings. Do not manufacture review schema, awards, ratings or FAQ rich-result expectations.

### Practical SEO rollout

- Give each service a distinct canonical URL and primary intent; keep management synonyms on one strong page rather than near-duplicate doorway pages.
- Use US English throughout, naturally recognize the UK spelling in relevant article context, and create country pages only when unique local expertise and service evidence exist.
- Ship server-rendered titles/descriptions, one page-level H1, descriptive internal links, sitemap and robots rules; validate the final production domain before indexing.
- Add accurate organization/service/article structured data only where the visible content supports it. No fake reviews or invented address.
- Verify Search Console after domain ownership, submit sitemap, and review impressions/queries/leads before expanding the content plan.
- Measure qualified inquiries, service-to-contact clicks and successful delivery after explicit analytics configuration and appropriate consent. Report observed performance, never a promised Lighthouse score.

## Evidence gaps to close after launch

The client must approve the legal brand/domain, delivery capabilities, named team, actual markets served, testimonials and all client permissions. Competitor desktop scores here cannot establish mobile UX or performance. A deeper competitive audit would require rendered full-page desktop/mobile captures for the text-only sites, task-based navigation tests and controlled lab measurements. These missing checks are recorded rather than represented as completed.
